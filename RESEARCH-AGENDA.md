# Research agenda

[Back to Fajrix Research](README.md) · [Publication register](RESEARCH-REGISTER.md) · [Research approach](RESEARCH-APPROACH.md)

This agenda describes research questions Fajrix considers worth investigating. It is a direction of travel, not a list of completed studies or a commitment to deliver every example below. The [research register](RESEARCH-REGISTER.md) identifies what has actually been released.

## 01 — Financial intelligence and accountable automation

**Central question:** How can intelligent systems improve financial understanding and reduce repetitive work while preserving accounting correctness, traceability and human responsibility?

Questions for investigation:

- How accurately can financial documents be interpreted when source material is incomplete, inconsistent, multilingual or ambiguous?
- Which parts of accounting classification should be deterministic, which might benefit from model-assisted analysis, and when must a qualified person review the decision?
- How can a recommendation be traced to source documents, accounting policy, assumptions and calculations?
- How should evaluation account for monetary impact, error severity, false confidence and the cost of manual verification—not just aggregate accuracy?
- How should the rules of a jurisdiction, reporting context or accounting policy constrain a proposed treatment?

**Possible outputs:** Controlled document evaluations using permitted or synthetic inputs, worked accounting scenarios, error taxonomies and comparisons of review strategies. Such examples would not by themselves establish suitability for real financial reporting.

## 02 — Transaction integrity and resilient systems

**Central question:** How can financial workflows preserve intended outcomes and provide reliable evidence when components fail or confirmations are lost?

Questions for investigation:

- What evidence distinguishes a transaction that was not submitted, one that did not commit and one that committed but whose acknowledgement was lost?
- How can idempotency, concurrency controls, audit evidence and recovery mechanisms prevent duplicate financial effects?
- What should remain available when cloud providers, networks, identity systems or supporting services fail?
- How can deployment provenance, dependency assurance, access controls and incident recovery be tested rather than assumed?
- Which operational claims can be supported by a controlled experiment, and which require production-specific acceptance or longer-term observation?

**Possible outputs:** Reproducible failure scenarios, synthetic transaction experiments, recovery protocols and threat-informed assurance notes. A laboratory result is not a production availability guarantee.

## 03 — Trustworthy AI and evaluation

**Central question:** How should AI-assisted financial workflows be evaluated as complete systems, including their tools, permissions, data and review mechanisms?

Questions for investigation:

- Can a model's advice be kept separate from verified calculations and authorised financial actions?
- Which evaluation techniques reveal unsupported explanations, stale inputs, inconsistent outputs and harmful automation errors?
- What must be retested when a model, prompt, retrieval source, orchestration framework or provider changes?
- How can private information, identity boundaries and least-privilege tool access be protected across the entire workflow?
- When do model outputs improve a user's decision compared with an auditable deterministic baseline, after accounting for cost, latency and review?

**Possible outputs:** Evaluation protocols, restricted tool-use demonstrations, model-comparison methods and failure-case catalogues. Demonstrations must disclose which results are measured and which are illustrative.

## 04 — Strategic finance and AI capability

**Central question:** How do financial systems, technological capability, adoption and strategic dependencies affect economic resilience and business opportunity?

Questions for investigation:

- How do financial workflows and regulatory contexts differ across the UK, European and Middle Eastern markets, and what can be compared responsibly?
- How should businesses assess concentration risks across technology, identity, communications and infrastructure providers?
- What can investment, compute access, talent, adoption, ownership and domestic value capture each tell us about national AI capability?
- Under what conditions are international technology-policy lessons transferable to the UK?
- How can strategic analysis distinguish reported evidence, scenarios and policy preferences?

**Possible outputs:** Dated source comparisons, jurisdiction-specific questions, scenario analyses, country profiles and policy commentary. A comparative study must define its periods, units, coverage, attribution and uncertainty.

## How we select research

We favour questions that meet five tests:

1. **Decision relevance:** A clear audience can use the answer to understand a meaningful problem.
2. **Answerable scope:** The claim can be examined using an appropriate method, with limitations made explicit.
3. **Evidence and provenance:** Data, references, calculations and permissions can be traced and reviewed.
4. **Failure awareness:** The study can discuss counterexamples, severity, uncertainty and alternative explanations.
5. **Responsible publication:** Results can be shared without exposing private material, creating false assurance or implying unavailable product capabilities.

The first three programmes are particularly close to Fajrix's financial technology and engineering direction. Strategic finance and national AI capability offer a complementary economic and policy perspective.

## From question to publication

A programme heading is not a study status. Specific proposals, work in progress and published artefacts are tracked separately in the [research register](RESEARCH-REGISTER.md). Every published study should use the [research approach](RESEARCH-APPROACH.md) and an appropriately completed [study companion](templates/STUDY-COMPANION.md).

The agenda may evolve as evidence and priorities change. We will describe substantive changes rather than quietly reframe earlier findings.
